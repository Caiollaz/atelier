# Library

Read this when you need references: to set a direction, to see how shipped products solve a pattern, or to back a decision with a source. Take qualities (density, restraint, type discipline, how a moment is placed), never a company's identity, screens or assets. Links were checked in November 2026; galleries change, so verify before citing.

## How to use references

1. Start from the surface and the direction (`direction.md`), then look for products with the same job, not the same industry look.
2. Prefer shipped products over concept shots. If no real product does something, treat it as art direction, not as a UI spec.
3. Collect three to five references and write, for each, the one quality you are taking ("dense tables with quiet headers", "one serif only on the hero").
4. Never copy layouts wholesale, and never reuse logos, illustrations, photography or proprietary fonts.

### Dribbble and Behance without the concept slop

They are useful for mood and art direction, risky as specs. Most shots are unshipped concepts with invented data.
- Search for a real company's team account or a product name, not generic terms like "fintech dashboard".
- Discard shots with: round vanity numbers, gradient or glass on every card, icon-in-circle KPI rows, a navy sidebar with a colored pill, devices floating at angles, lorem ipsum or "Jane Doe".
- Cross-check any idea against a shipped equivalent on Mobbin, Refero or Page Flows before adopting it.
- Use color search to explore palettes, then check contrast; shot palettes are rarely accessible.
- On Behance, prefer case studies that link to the live product and show research or before and after.

## Design systems to learn from

Patterns observed in public design systems and sites. Values are approximate and change; treat them as evidence of a decision, not as specs to paste.

| System | Decision worth borrowing |
|---|---|
| Linear | Elevation on dark from white at 2 to 5% opacity steps; a between weight (around 510) instead of bold; no pure white text |
| Vercel (Geist) | A 1px ring shadow instead of a border; tight display tracking that relaxes to normal at body size; workflow colors only for pipeline stages |
| Stripe | A two-layer shadow (far and near) tinted toward the brand; light-weight large headlines; tabular numerals everywhere money appears |
| Notion | Warm whites in alternating bands for rhythm; very low-opacity layered shadows; documented hover, active and disabled states |
| Apple | Product on a solid color field; section color as rhythm; one blue only for interactive things |
| IBM Carbon | Inputs with a bottom border only; 48px control height; layering by surface color instead of shadow; one blue accent |
| Supabase | Elevation from border steps on dark instead of shadows; one green used sparingly |
| Raycast | Hover by opacity, not color; key caps with layered shadows |
| Sanity | One universal hover color as the activation signal |
| Sentry | Glass blur used only for real overlays, not content cards |
| PostHog | The brand color hidden until hover; a hand-drawn mascot as identity |
| HashiCorp | Each product owns exactly one color |
| Superhuman | One dramatic color gesture, restraint everywhere else; only two radii |
| Ollama | Radical restraint: two radii, zero shadows, grayscale |
| OpenCode | One monospace face for everything; a single button variant |
| Mistral | Emphasis by size and color, never by weight |
| Cohere | One signature radius as identity |
| Wise | A heavy display face at very tight leading; scale up on hover and down on press for playful controls |
| Uber | Black primary and white secondary as a pair; dense, compact pages |
| Material 3 | Window size classes (compact under 600dp, medium to 840, expanded to 1200) for responsive decisions |
| Radix Colors | Twelve steps per color, each with one job: backgrounds 1 and 2, component fills 3 to 5, borders 6 to 8, solid 9 and 10, text 11 and 12 |

Notice the spread: some excellent systems use zero shadows, some use layered ones; some use pills everywhere, some never. What they share is that every choice is made once and kept.

## Guidance sources

| Source | Most actionable points |
|---|---|
| [Vercel Web Interface Guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) | Visible focus with `:focus-visible`; hit target at least 24px (44 on mobile); 16px inputs on mobile; never disable zoom; loading buttons keep their label; show a loader after about 150 to 300ms and keep it at least 300 to 500ms; URL as state; confirm or undo destructive actions; tooltip delay only on the first in a group; links are links; `aria-live` for async updates; never `transition: all`; tabular numbers; curly quotes and the ellipsis character; errors next to fields and focus the first on submit; layered shadows; nested radii; `color-scheme: dark`; `theme-color` matches the background |
| [Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/) | iOS default text 17pt, minimum 11pt; text contrast 4.5:1 (3:1 for 18pt or bold); controls 44x44pt default, 28x28pt minimum; avoid light font weights; Dynamic Type scale from 11 to 34pt |
| [Android accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps) | Touch targets at least 48x48dp; contrast 4.5:1 and 3:1 for large text; window size classes for layout |
| [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/) | 1.4.3 contrast 4.5:1, large text 3:1 (ratios are not rounded); 2.5.8 targets at least 24x24 CSS px or spaced |
| [APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html) | Lc 90 preferred for body, Lc 75 minimum for body at 16px/500 or 18px/400, Lc 60 other content, Lc 45 large headlines and icons, Lc 30 placeholder and disabled, Lc 15 dividers; better than WCAG 2 for dark mode |
| [Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html) | Body 15 to 25px on the web; line spacing 120 to 145%; 45 to 90 characters per line; caps get 5 to 12% letterspacing; centered text sparingly; real dashes and ellipsis |
| [Every Layout](https://every-layout.dev/rudiments/axioms/) | Express rules as axioms the browser computes, such as `max-inline-size: 60ch` on flow elements |
| [Utopia](https://utopia.fyi) | Fluid type and space scales interpolated between a phone scale and a desktop scale |
| [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com) | Doherty threshold (respond under 400ms), Fitts, Hick, Jakob, proximity, common region, Von Restorff, peak-end |
| [Nielsen Norman Group heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) | System status, real-world match, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, efficiency, minimalist design, error recovery, help |
| [Invisible Details of Interaction Design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design) | No animation on high-frequency keyboard actions; interruptible motion; responsive gestures apply the delta immediately; momentum keeps velocity; elements enter from where they live |
| [Designing Beautiful Shadows](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows/) | One light source; offsets in a fixed ratio; blur grows with elevation; layer shadows and tint them toward the background |
| [Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com) | Start with a feature, not a layout; hierarchy is everything; greys don't have to be grey; use fewer borders; shadows convey elevation |
| [Emil Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski) and [animations.dev](https://animations.dev) | Motion craft: frequency decides whether to animate, ease-out for responsiveness, under 300ms, interruptible |
| [unslop](https://heydamir.design/unslop) | Turning AI-looking screens into designed ones, pass by pass |
| [taste-skill](https://www.tasteskill.dev) | Anti-slop rules backed by a study of award-winning and weak sites; real-browser checks |

## When sources disagree

They often do. Shadows, borders, monospace labels, uppercase eyebrows, pills and violet are all used well by some excellent products and badly by most generated ones. atelier's position (see the non-negotiables in `SKILL.md`): a pattern is a tell when it is an unchosen default applied everywhere; it is a choice when the brand uses it deliberately, sparingly and consistently. When you keep a "tell" on purpose, say why in the report.
